Five tests. The weakest one decides.

Most checklists average everything out. A side hustle doesn't work that way. It breaks at its weakest point, so one zero caps your verdict no matter what else scored well.

  1. Already has buyers

    Can you point to strangers who already pay for something like it? And why would they pick you?

  2. Already has a door

    Is there a place buyers already look, so you don't have to build an audience first?

  3. Fits your week

    Do you have the hours, and what happens if it works better than you planned?

  4. Safe to be wrong

    What is the most you could lose and still sleep fine? Could you stop in a weekend?

  5. The math holds

    Do your own numbers add up to the extra income you're aiming for, in the hours you really have?

Five hard stops come first.

Before any scoring, five yes or no questions flag what deserves a hard look. Any yes means pause and think it through.

  • Your work agreement might not allow it
  • It needs you reachable during work hours
  • It needs a license, insurance, or equipment that's costly or hard to get
  • It costs more than you could afford to lose
  • The people at home don't know yet
