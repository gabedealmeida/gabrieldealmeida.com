How I think about it

The Automation Ladder. Climb it in order.

Start at the bottom

  1. Assist

    AI drafts. You decide and send.

  2. Automate a task

    One trigger, one job, done without you.

  3. Automate a workflow

    Several steps, with checks in between.

  4. Delegate with guardrails

    AI handles open-ended work, inside limits you set.

Quick win, low riskMore power, more oversight

Trust is earned.

Each rung proves the one below it works.

Mistakes stay small.

You find the problems on small tasks, not big ones.

You stay in control.

You decide how far to go, and when to stop.

Questions you might have.

Is AI automation just ChatGPT?

No. A chat tool helps when you ask it. An automation runs on its own when something happens, like a new email or a form, and does the same job every time. AI is one step inside it, and often not the biggest step.

What kind of business is this for?

Any business with repeating work, like follow-ups, reporting, scheduling or customer questions. If it is a fit, we start with one task.

What should I automate first?

Something that happens every week, follows clear steps, costs little if it goes wrong, and can be measured. The four tests above sort that out quickly.

Is it safe to put customer data into AI?

It depends on the tool, the settings and the data. I check what a workflow touches before building it, and I keep sensitive steps in front of a person. This is not legal or security advice.

Will it make mistakes?

Yes, sometimes. That is why anything that touches money, customers or compliance gets a human check, and why I start small.

Will this replace my team?

I build automations to take over tasks, not to replace people. The aim is to give your team its time back for the work that needs a person.

Do I need to be technical?

No. If you can describe the task and show me how it is done today, that is enough to start.
