# Score My Side Hustle Idea

**Will this side hustle fit around your job?**
A scorecard for people who are keeping their job and starting a side hustle.

DRAFT v1 for Gabe's review. Voice rules applied: plain words, "side hustle," no em dashes, no income promises, no employer named. Lines marked "From my misses" are only things Gabe has said himself.

---

## How this works (page 1)

Grab a pencil. This takes about 15 minutes.

1. Write the **smallest version** of your idea.
2. Check the **five hard stops.** Any "yes" means pause.
3. Score the **five tests.**
4. Read your **verdict.**
5. Run the **one-page test** on your weakest spot, this week.

Four rules:

- **Score only what you can show.** A name, a number, a link, a date. If you can't show it, it's a 0. A 0 isn't failure. It tells you what to find out.
- **The weakest test decides.** An idea breaks at its weakest point, so we don't average.
- **Score the smallest version, not the dream.** The same idea can be a great side hustle at one size and a second job at another.
- **This is guidance, not a rulebook.** You know your situation better than any scorecard does. If you decide to go against a stop or a verdict, that can be a fine call. Just write down why, and what would prove you wrong.

*From my wins: I've started things that cost more than I'd normally risk, because the demand was already proven, the distribution was in place, and clients were lined up. This scorecard would have flagged the cost. The evidence is why I went ahead.*

*From my misses: I once ran a pool cleaning service. A handful of clients before work, after work, and on weekends would have been a great side hustle. It got too big, too fast. The week stopped belonging to the job.*

## Step 1. Shrink it (page 3)

Write the smallest version of this idea you could run on weekends. Everything below is scored against this version.

Idea name: ______________________

- **I will** (what you do): ______________________
- **for** (who pays you): ______________________
- **so they** (what they get): ______________________
- **on** (which days): ______________________
- **Starting with no more than** ______ **customers.**

Example: *I will clean pools for neighbors within 10 minutes of my house, on Saturday mornings, starting with no more than 5 customers.*

**What does the smallest version need?** Tick everything that applies. Fewer is better.

- [ ] Hiring or managing other people
- [ ] A license, permit, or insurance
- [ ] Equipment or tools you would have to buy
- [ ] Inventory or supplies you would have to stock
- [ ] A space to rent or store things
- [ ] A website, app, or store you would have to build
- [ ] A business entity, separate bank account, or tax setup
- [ ] Fixed appointments or deadlines you cannot move

Ticked: ______. 0 to 2 is a light start. 3 to 4 is getting heavy, so can you drop one? 5 or more, shrink it again before you score it.

## Step 2. The five hard stops (page 3)

Answer yes or no. **Any yes means pause and think it through.** Usually that means fix it first, then come back. A hard stop is a "not yet," not a "no forever."

If you decide to go ahead anyway, that's allowed. Use the box under the table to say why.

| | Hard stop | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Your work agreement.** Might your employment agreement or an outside-work policy rule this out, or have you not checked yet? Look for outside work, conflicts of interest, and who owns what you create. (A planning tool, not legal advice.) | | |
| 2 | **Reachable 9 to 5.** Does it need you reachable on weekdays during work hours? Calls, live support, same-day replies, showing up in person. | | |
| 3 | **Hard-to-get requirements.** Does it need a license, insurance, or equipment that is costly or hard to get? Basic tools you already own don't count. | | |
| 4 | **Your 2 a.m. number.** Write the most you could lose without touching your family's cushion: $________. Could finding out if this works cost more than that, after subtracting money customers have already committed (deposits, signed clients, paid orders)? | | |
| 5 | **Home.** Does anyone who would feel the hours or the dollars not know yet, or not agree? | | |

**Going ahead anyway?** If you answered yes to any stop and still want to start, write it down.

Which stop: ______ Why I'm going ahead: ______________________________________________

What would prove me wrong: ______________________________________________

*Strong evidence can justify a bigger bet. Real buyers, a real door, and clients lined up (Tests 1 and 2) make a stop 3 or 4 yes much less scary. Thin evidence doesn't.*

*From my misses: the licenses, insurance, and equipment piled up on my pool business. I could have gotten past it by staying lean. I got over my skis instead.*

## Step 3. The five tests (pages 4 to 7)

Each question scores **0, 1, or 2.** Each test totals 0 to 6.

### Test 1. Already has buyers
*Pick a market that already pays.*

**1a. Have you seen strangers actually pay for something like this?**
- 0: No. I think people would want it, or only friends and family say they would.
- 1: I've seen it sold, but couldn't tell how often.
- 2: Yes, and I can show it. At least 3 recent sales or dated reviews from strangers.

**1b. Have you asked a stranger for money?**
- 0: Never asked.
- 1: I've asked friends or family.
- 2: I've asked strangers. Even if they said no, I know why.

**1c. Why would a stranger pick you over what's already there?**
- 0: I'd be about the same as everyone else.
- 1: I'd be cheaper or more convenient, but anyone could match that.
- 2: I can name one specific thing that's different and hard to copy, in one sentence.

*From my misses: I started a fitness company in a crowded space with nothing that set me apart. I couldn't get people to pay.*
*Note: if you'd be competing with people who have far more money, information, or time than you (trading is one), your 1c is probably a 0.*

### Test 2. Already has a door
*Sell where buyers already are.*

**2a. Is there a place buyers already go to find this?** A marketplace, search, local groups, a platform.
- 0: I'd have to build an audience or a website first.
- 1: Maybe. I know a place but haven't tried it.
- 2: Yes. I can name the place and I've seen buyers asking or buying there.

**2b. Could you get your first 10 customers with zero followers?**
- 0: Not without ads, an audience, or luck.
- 1: Probably, but I'd have to learn a new channel first.
- 2: Yes. I can describe the steps in 5 lines or fewer.

**2c. If that door closed, would you have another?**
- 0: No. The whole thing runs through one platform or one person.
- 1: I could find another, but it would take months.
- 2: Yes. I already have a second way in, or it doesn't depend on one platform.

*From my misses: with the fitness company I was starting from the ground up, trying to get people to a website. There was no door already open.*

### Test 3. Fits your week
*The week belongs to the job.*

**3a. Do the hours fit?** Honest hours you can protect each week: ______. Hours your smallest version needs: ______.
- 0: It needs more than I have, or I'm not sure.
- 1: It needs about what I have, with no slack.
- 2: It needs no more than two thirds of what I have.

**3b. If you skipped a week (sick kid, big deadline), what happens?**
- 0: Customers leave or things pile up.
- 1: Some pain, but it recovers.
- 2: Nothing breaks.

**3c. What if it works too well?** If it doubled next month, what happens?
- 0: I couldn't keep up and I'd have no way to say no.
- 1: I'd manage for a while but I have no plan.
- 2: I can cap it. A waitlist, a customer limit, or a price that slows demand.

*From my misses: my pool business worked, then got too big too quick and collided with my job. I also ran a home wholesaling business. It needed a bigger operation and more of my attention than I had.*

### Test 4. Safe to be wrong
*Your 2 a.m. number.* (Copy it from hard stop 4: $________)

**4a. What would it cost to find out if this works?** Include the one-page test and the first month. Subtract money customers have already committed (deposits, signed clients, paid orders).
- 0: More than my 2 a.m. number, or I don't know.
- 1: Within it, but I'd feel it.
- 2: Less than half of it.

**4b. Could you stop in one weekend and owe nothing?** No leases, inventory you can't sell back, contracts, or equipment loans.
- 0: No. I'd be locked into something.
- 1: Mostly. One or two things to unwind.
- 2: Yes. I could stop Sunday night and owe nothing.

**4c. Could you watch the swings without losing sleep?**
- 0: It uses my family's cushion, or I'd check it constantly.
- 1: It's my own spare money, but I'd watch it closely.
- 2: I'd be fine if it went to zero, and I wouldn't need to check daily.

*From my misses: I dabbled in stock trading. The ups and downs were more than I could stomach, it was my family's money, and the people on the other side had far more resources than I did.*

### Test 5. The math holds
*Your napkin. This is your own arithmetic, not a forecast.*

| | |
|---|---|
| A. Aim for, per month | $________ |
| B. Profit per sale (price, minus what it costs you to deliver, including fees) | $________ |
| C. Sales needed per month (A ÷ B) | ________ |
| D. Hours per sale (finding the customer, the work, the admin) | ________ |
| E. Hours needed per month (C × D) | ________ |
| F. Hours you can protect per month (weekly hours from 3a × 4) | ________ |

**5a. Do the hours fit?**
- 0: E is more than F.
- 1: E is up to F, with little room.
- 2: E is no more than three quarters of F.

**5b. Is the profit per sale real?**
- 0: A guess.
- 1: Estimated from similar sellers.
- 2: From costs I've actually priced out (materials, fees, delivery, my time).

**5c. Does one source carry the plan?** One customer, one platform, or one supplier.
- 0: One source is more than half.
- 1: One source is about half.
- 2: No source is more than a third.

*One paycheck is a single point of failure. Don't build a second one.*

## Step 4. Your verdict (page 8)

Write each test's score (0 to 6) and circle the lowest.

| Test 1 | Test 2 | Test 3 | Test 4 | Test 5 | Total (out of 30) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

Read your verdict from the **lowest** test:

- **Lowest is 4 or more: Go. Start Saturday.** It passed. Pick a start date and keep the first version small.
- **Lowest is 2 or 3: Fix one thing first.** One weak spot. Change it, then rescore that test.
- **Lowest is 0 or 1: Prove it before you spend.** Run the one-page test on that test before you put in money or weekends.
- **Two or more tests at 0 or 1: Not this one. Next idea.** Better to know on day one than in month six.

Any hard stop with a yes? Fix that first, or write down why you're going ahead anyway. The verdict doesn't count until you've dealt with the stops.

**A verdict is a recommendation, not a ruling.** If your gut and the scorecard disagree, don't ignore either one. Find out why. Then decide.

## Step 5. The one-page test (page 9)

Run **only the one for your weakest test.** If two are tied, start with the lower number. Keep it under $25 and one week. Then rescore that test.

| Weakest test | This week, do this | Pass if |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Buyers** | Write a one-line offer with a price. Put it where buyers already look, or message 10 people who recently bought something similar. Ask 5 for a real commitment, such as a booked time or a pre-order. | At least 2 say yes with a date or money, or you learn exactly why not. |
| **2. Door** | Post one real offer on the place buyers already go. Count messages for 7 days, not likes. | At least 3 real inquiries from strangers. |
| **3. Week** | Run a mock Saturday. Do one customer-sized job in the real time slot you'd use. Time every step, including admin. Note what interrupts you. | It took no more than the hours you budgeted, and the rest of your week didn't suffer. |
| **4. Safe** | Write your 2 a.m. number. Spend 15 minutes with the person at home: the idea, the hours, the most you'll risk, and what would make them uneasy. List every contract, lease, or purchase and check each can be undone. | You both agree on the number, and nothing on your list can't be undone. |
| **5. Math** | Replace one guess with a real number. Get one real price (a quote or a competitor's published price) and one real cost. Redo the napkin. | The hours needed fit within the hours you have, using real numbers. |

After the week, rescore that test. If it's now 4 or more, check your next-lowest test.
