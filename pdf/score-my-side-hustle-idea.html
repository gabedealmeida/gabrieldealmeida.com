Score My Side Hustle IdeaGabe De Almeida

How this works.

Grab a pencil. This takes about 15 minutes. For every question, fill in the circle that fits best.

  1. 1Shrink it. Write the smallest version of your idea.
  2. 2Check the hard stops. Five yes or no questions. Any yes means pause and think.
  3. 3Score the five tests. Three questions each, scored 0, 1, or 2.
  4. 4Read your verdict. It comes from your weakest test.
  5. 5Run the one-page test. On your weakest spot, this week.

Score only what you can show.

A name, a number, a link, a date. If you can't show it, it's a 0. A 0 isn't failure. It tells you what to find out.

The weakest test decides.

An idea breaks at its weakest point, so we don't average.

Score the smallest version, not the dream.

The same idea can be a great side hustle at one size and a second job at another.

This is guidance, not a rulebook.

You know your situation better than any scorecard does. If you go against a stop or a verdict, that can be a fine call. Write down why, and what would prove you wrong.

From my misses. I once ran a pool cleaning service. A handful of clients before work, after work, and on weekends would have been a great side hustle. It got too big, too fast. The week stopped belonging to the job.
From my wins. I've started things that cost more than I'd normally risk, because the demand was already proven, the distribution was in place, and clients were lined up. This scorecard would have flagged the cost. The evidence is why I went ahead.

Step 2

Five hard stops.

Answer yes or no. Any yes means pause and think it through. Usually that means fix it first, then come back. A hard stop is a "not yet," not a "no forever."

Hard stopYesNo
1 Your work agreement.

Might your employment agreement or an outside-work policy rule this out, or have you not checked yet? Look for outside work, conflicts of interest, and who owns what you create. This is a planning tool, not legal advice.
2 Reachable 9 to 5.

Does it need you reachable on weekdays during work hours? Calls, live support, same-day replies, showing up in person.
3 Hard-to-get requirements.

Does it need a license, insurance, or equipment that is costly or hard to get? Basic tools you already own don't count.
4 Your 2 a.m. number.

The most you could lose without touching your family's cushion: $
Could finding out if this works cost more than that, after subtracting money customers have already committed (deposits, signed clients, paid orders)?
5 Home.

Does anyone who would feel the hours or the dollars not know yet, or not agree?

Going ahead anyway?

Strong evidence can justify a bigger bet. Real buyers, a real door, and clients lined up (Tests 1 and 2) make a stop 3 or 4 yes much less scary. Thin evidence doesn't.

From my misses. The licenses, insurance, and equipment piled up on my pool business. I could have gotten past it by staying lean. I got over my skis instead.

Test 1Already has buyers

Pick a market that already pays.

Test total/ 6

1a. Have you seen strangers actually pay for something like this?

0No. I think people would want it, or only friends and family say they would.
1I've seen it sold, but couldn't tell how often.
2Yes, and I can show it. At least 3 recent sales or dated reviews from strangers.

1b. Have you asked a stranger for money?

0Never asked.
1I've asked friends or family.
2I've asked strangers. Even if they said no, I know why.

1c. Why would a stranger pick you over what's already there?

0I'd be about the same as everyone else.
1I'd be cheaper or more convenient, but anyone could match that.
2I can name one specific thing that's different and hard to copy, in one sentence.
From my misses. I started a fitness company in a crowded space with nothing that set me apart. I couldn't get people to pay. If you'd be competing with people who have far more money, information, or time than you (trading is one), your 1c is probably a 0.

Test 2Already has a door

Sell where buyers already are.

Test total/ 6

2a. Is there a place buyers already go to find this? A marketplace, search, local groups, a platform.

0I'd have to build an audience or a website first.
1Maybe. I know a place but haven't tried it.
2Yes. I can name the place and I've seen buyers asking or buying there.

2b. Could you get your first 10 customers with zero followers?

0Not without ads, an audience, or luck.
1Probably, but I'd have to learn a new channel first.
2Yes. I can describe the steps in 5 lines or fewer.

2c. If that door closed, would you have another?

0No. The whole thing runs through one platform or one person.
1I could find another, but it would take months.
2Yes. I already have a second way in, or it doesn't depend on one platform.
From my misses. With the fitness company I was starting from the ground up, trying to get people to a website. There was no door already open.

Test 3Fits your week

The week belongs to the job.

Test total/ 6

3a. Do the hours fit?

Honest hours I can protect each week: Hours my smallest version needs:
0It needs more than I have, or I'm not sure.
1It needs about what I have, with no slack.
2It needs no more than two thirds of what I have.

3b. If you skipped a week (sick kid, big deadline), what happens?

0Customers leave or things pile up.
1Some pain, but it recovers.
2Nothing breaks.

3c. What if it works too well? If it doubled next month, what happens?

0I couldn't keep up and I'd have no way to say no.
1I'd manage for a while but I have no plan.
2I can cap it. A waitlist, a customer limit, or a price that slows demand.
From my misses. My pool business worked, then got too big too quick and collided with my job. I also ran a home wholesaling business. It needed a bigger operation and more of my attention than I had. Check what you ticked in Step 1.

Test 4Safe to be wrong

Your 2 a.m. number: $

Test total/ 6

4a. What would it cost to find out if this works? Include the one-page test and the first month, minus money customers already committed.

0More than my 2 a.m. number, or I don't know.
1Within it, but I'd feel it.
2Less than half of it.

4b. Could you stop in one weekend and owe nothing? No leases, unsellable inventory, contracts, or loans.

0No. I'd be locked into something.
1Mostly. One or two things to unwind.
2Yes. I could stop Sunday night and owe nothing.

4c. Could you watch the swings without losing sleep?

0It uses my family's cushion, or I'd check it constantly.
1It's my own spare money, but I'd watch it closely.
2I'd be fine if it went to zero, and I wouldn't need to check daily.
From my misses. I dabbled in stock trading. The ups and downs were more than I could stomach, it was my family's money, and the people on the other side had far more resources than I did.

0E is more than F.
1E is up to F, with little room.

5b. Is the profit per sale real?

0A guess.
1Estimated from similar sellers.
2From costs I've actually priced out (materials, fees, delivery, my time).

5c. Does one source carry the plan? One customer, one platform, or one supplier.

0One source is more than half.
1One source is about half.
2No source is more than a third.
One paycheck is a single point of failure. Don't build a second one. The math can tell you whether an idea works on paper. It can't tell you whether it will.

Step 5

The one-page test.

Run only the one for your weakest test. If two are tied, start with the lower number. Keep it under $25 and one week. Then rescore that test.

Weakest test

1. Buyers

This week, do this

Write a one-line offer with a price. Put it where buyers already look, or message 10 people who recently bought something similar. Ask 5 for a real commitment, such as a booked time or a pre-order.

Pass if

At least 2 say yes with a date or money, or you learn exactly why not.

Weakest test

2. Door

This week, do this

Post one real offer on the place buyers already go. Count messages for 7 days, not likes.

Pass if

At least 3 real inquiries from strangers.

Weakest test

3. Week

This week, do this

Run a mock Saturday. Do one customer-sized job in the real time slot you'd use. Time every step, including admin. Note what interrupts you.

Pass if

It took no more than the hours you budgeted, and the rest of your week didn't suffer.

Weakest test

4. Safe

This week, do this

Write your 2 a.m. number. Spend 15 minutes with the person at home: the idea, the hours, the most you'll risk, and what would make them uneasy. List every contract, lease, or purchase and check that each can be undone.

Pass if

You both agree on the number, and nothing on your list can't be undone.

Weakest test

5. Math

This week, do this

Replace one guess with a real number. Get one real price (a quote or a competitor's published price) and one real cost. Redo the napkin.

Pass if

The hours needed fit within the hours you have, using real numbers.

After the week, rescore that test. If it's now 4 or more, check your next-lowest test.
